Answer Hypothetical Questions

In this type of question, the interviewer asks you to imagine a situation that is not real ("If you were…?", "Imagine…") and say what you would do or choose in it, and why. It belongs to the group of abstract and speculative questions in the Take an Interview task.

GroupAbstract and speculative questions
Where in the interviewLater. According to the Official Guide, later questions ask you to express opinions, speculate or respond to hypothetical situations.
Official example"Imagine you're unclear about a concept in class… would you ask a classmate or your professor? Why?"
Time45 seconds, no preparation
Scoring0–5, the Take an Interview scoring guide (relevance, elaboration, delivery, language use)

How to Recognize It

The Official Guide labels this type (If you were…?). The question sets up an imagined situation, then asks what you would do. Typical wordings:

  • Imagine … What would you do?
  • If you were …, would you … or …? Why?
  • If you could …, what would you choose?
  • Suppose … How would you …?
  • What would you do if …?
  • If you had to …, which would you pick?

Like the official example, many of these questions give you a choice and ask why. Both parts need an answer.

The Scoring Guide, Applied to This Question Type

The four dimensions of the Take an Interview scoring guide mean the following for an answer to a hypothetical question:

DimensionStronger answer (4–5)Weaker answer (2–3)
RelevanceStays inside the imagined situation, makes a clear choice and explains whyAvoids the choice ("It depends" and nothing more), or talks about a different situation
ElaborationGives reasons, describes what would happen as a result, and may support it with a real experienceA choice with no support: "I would ask my professor."
DeliveryA steady conversational pace; pauses sound naturalFrequent or long pauses, a choppy pace, many fillers
Language useAccurate conditional forms kept consistent through the answerLimited grammar and vocabulary clearly restrict what can be said

Grammar for Imagined Situations

Hypothetical answers are built on the second conditional: unreal or imagined situations in the present or future. Keeping it accurate for 45 seconds is a real test of language use:

PatternUseExample
If + past simple, would + verbThe basic structureIf I had a free day, I would visit my grandparents.
If I were …were for all persons in formal useIf I were in charge, I'd change the schedule.
I'd / I wouldn'tNatural spoken contractionsI'd probably ask a classmate first.
could / might instead of wouldPossibility instead of certaintyI might feel nervous, but I could learn a lot.
would rather / would prefer toMaking the choiceI'd rather live with roommates.
That way, … / Otherwise, …Consequences inside the imagined situationI'd tell my manager right away. Otherwise, the problem would get worse.
Linking to real lifeSupporting the choice with experienceI did something similar last year, so I know it would help.

Common conditional errors

  • If I would have more time… → If I had more time… (no would in the if-part)
  • If I will be a teacher… → If I were a teacher… (an imagined situation, not a real future one)
  • …I will choose the professor. → …I would choose the professor. (keep would through the whole answer)

Official Guide strategies that matter most here

  • Answer the question directly. Make sure your response addresses the specific question asked; if it offers a choice, make your choice clear.
  • Explain your reasoning. Elaborate with details, examples or reasoning: why would you make that choice, and what would happen?
  • If the situation is unfamiliar, draw on your general experience. Don't let an unusual scenario distract you.
  • Use the full 45 seconds. If you finish early, add more details or examples; if time is running out, move on to your next point.
  • Vary your vocabulary and sentence structures, and keep going after a mistake.
  • No memorized language, including prepared introductions and conclusions.

Practice: 8 Hypothetical Questions

Two sets of four questions, all of this type, in test conditions: each question is asked once, and your 45 seconds start right after it. Afterwards, listen to your answers and check them against the scoring guide. The questions were written for this platform in the style of the task; they are not official test questions.

The interactive simulator needs JavaScript. You can still practice with a partner: they read each question once, and you answer for 45 seconds with a timer.

Research study: campus life: questions for your partner

You are taking part in a research study about campus life. A researcher will ask you to imagine some situations.

  1. Thanks for joining. Imagine your university gave every student one free day a week with no classes. How would you spend it? Why?
  2. Thank you. If you could add one new building or facility to your campus, what would it be? Why?
  3. Interesting. Suppose you had to choose between living alone and living with roommates for a year. Which would you choose, and why?
  4. Good. Imagine you were in charge of welcoming new international students. What would you do to help them feel at home?
Workplace research: questions for your partner

A research team is studying how young people think about work. An interviewer will ask you some questions.

  1. Hello, and thanks for taking part. Imagine you could work from anywhere in the world for a year. Where would you go, and why?
  2. Thank you. If you had to learn a completely new skill in one month, what would you choose, and how would you learn it?
  3. I see. Suppose a friend asked you to start a small business together. Would you agree? Why or why not?
  4. Last question. Imagine you made a serious mistake at work. Would you tell your manager right away, or try to fix it yourself first? Why?

Review

Recognize

Imagine… · If you were / could… · Suppose… · What would you do if… Later in the interview.

Include

A clear choice inside the imagined situation, reasons, consequences, and possibly a real experience as support.

Language

If + past, would · If I were · I'd · could / might · would rather · otherwise.
