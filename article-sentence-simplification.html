Sentence Simplification

In a Sentence Simplification question, one long sentence in the passage is highlighted. You choose the option that keeps its essential meaning in a shorter, simpler form. The right answer may use completely different words and grammar, but it must not change the meaning or lose anything important.

What the question tests

Academic writers pack several ideas into one sentence: a main statement, a reason, a contrast, a condition, an example, a side comment. This question checks whether you can see which parts are essential, which are extra, and how the parts are connected.

The question wording is nearly always the same, so you can recognize it immediately:

Which of the following sentences best expresses the essential information in the highlighted sentence? Incorrect choices change the meaning in important ways or leave out essential information.

Note the second sentence of the stem: it tells you exactly how wrong answers are built. They either change the meaning or leave out essential information.

Smart Tip Find the logical link first. If the highlighted sentence says A because B, the right answer must also say that B causes A, even if it uses since, as a result of or due to. Most wrong options break exactly this link: they reverse it, remove it, or invent a new one.

Strategies for Sentence Simplification Questions

Step 1: Strip the sentence to its core.

Ask: who or what is the sentence about, and what does it say about them? Ignore examples and side details for now.

Step 2: Name the relationship.

Is it cause and effect, contrast, condition, comparison, or time order? Write it as a formula in your head: can squeeze through BECAUSE no bones.

Step 3: Check each option for meaning.

Does the option keep the same relationship, in the same direction? Does it keep words like some, may, only, or does it make the claim stronger or weaker?

Step 4: Check each option for completeness.

Is anything essential missing? An option can be 100% true and still be wrong because it leaves out half of the idea.

Step 5: Do not choose the most similar wording.

The option that copies the most words from the original is often a trap. Judge by meaning, not by how familiar the words look.

Common Traps

  • Reversed logic. Cause and effect change places, or can becomes cannot.
  • Contrast lost. An although sentence becomes a simple list of two facts, or the two sides of a comparison are swapped.
  • Missing half. The option is true but drops the reason, condition or result.
  • Changed certainty. may becomes will; some becomes all.
  • A detail promoted. A side detail becomes the main point of the option.
Example 1: cause and result

Escape Artists of the Sea

Octopuses are famous escape artists. Aquariums have reported octopuses slipping out of their tanks through surprisingly small gaps. Because an octopus has no bones, it can squeeze through any gap wider than its beak, the only hard part of its body, even though the rest of the animal may be many times larger. Strong suckers along their arms also let them grip almost any surface.

1. Which of the following sentences best expresses the essential information in the highlighted sentence? Incorrect choices change the meaning in important ways or leave out essential information.

Answer + Explanation

Correct Answer: A

Explanation

Break the highlighted sentence into its parts: what an octopus can do (squeeze through a gap far smaller than its body), why (it has no bones) and the limit (the gap must be wider than the beak, its only hard part). Option A keeps all three and the because link between them. It uses different words and grammar, and that is fine.

  • Choice B – it invents a relationship the sentence never makes and contradicts it: the rest of the body is many times larger than the beak.
  • Choice C – it reverses the meaning: the sentence says an octopus can pass through small gaps.
  • Choice D – it is true, but it leaves out the reason and the limit, which are essential information.
Example 2: comparison

Highlighted sentence

Unlike earlier studies, which relied on what volunteers remembered eating, the new research measured diet directly by analyzing blood samples, making its results considerably more reliable.

1. Which of the following sentences best expresses the essential information in the highlighted sentence? Incorrect choices change the meaning in important ways or leave out essential information.

Answer + Explanation

Correct Answer: B

Explanation

The sentence compares two methods (memory vs. blood tests) and gives a conclusion (the new research is more reliable). B keeps the contrast, the two methods in the right order, and the conclusion.

  • Choice A – it gives the blood tests to the earlier studies, the wrong way round.
  • Choice C – it gives the memory method to the new research, which reverses the comparison.
  • Choice D – it is a general statement that leaves out the comparison and the conclusion; the sentence says nothing about "many studies."
