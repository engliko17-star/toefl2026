Select the Sentence

Select the Sentence questions name a paragraph and describe a piece of information, such as a reason, an example or a comparison. Your task is to find the one sentence in that paragraph that provides it. On the test you do not choose a letter: you click the sentence in the passage.

How it works

The question refers to a specific part of the passage, usually one paragraph with four sentences. Each of those four sentences can be selected. You click the one that answers the question, and it stays highlighted when you move on. In printed practice material, including the Official Guide, the sentences are marked with letters instead, and you choose a letter.

In the Official Guide this type appeared only with academic passages, and not in the two full practice tests, only in the practice sets. It is less frequent than Factual or Vocabulary questions, but you should know the format so it does not surprise you on test day.

What the questions ask for

The question describes the function of the sentence you need. These are the most common kinds of information:

The question asks for…Typical wordingWhat the right sentence does
a definition or explanation…that explains what … issays what a term means or how something works
an example…that gives an example of …names a specific case of a general idea
a cause or an effect…that explains why … / that describes the result of …gives a reason or a consequence
a comparison or contrast…that compares … with … / describes a differenceputs two things side by side
evidence for a claim…that provides evidence that …supports a statement with a fact, study or observation

Typical wording

  • Identify the sentence in paragraph 2 that explains why …
  • Identify the sentence in paragraph 3 that describes …
  • Identify the sentence in paragraph 1 that provides evidence that …

Strategies for Select the Sentence Questions

Step 1: Underline the function in the question.

Is the question asking for a reason, an example, a definition, a contrast, or evidence? Also note the topic: protection from predators, between flights.

Step 2: Label each of the four sentences.

Read the paragraph and give each sentence a two- or three-word label: A = cool air, B = avoid overheating, C = hawks / safety, D = rest and feed.

Step 3: Match both the function and the topic.

The right sentence does the job the question describes and is about the right topic. A sentence with the right key words but a different function is a trap.

Step 4: Check the neighbors.

Before you click, make sure no other sentence does the same job better. Sometimes one sentence introduces an idea and the next one actually explains it.

Common Traps

  • Key word match, wrong function. A sentence contains a word from the question but does a different job, for example it introduces the topic instead of explaining it.
  • The general sentence. The first sentence of the paragraph often states the topic. If the question asks for an example or a reason, the answer is usually a more specific sentence.
  • Half an answer. A sentence that mentions the topic but does not give the reason, effect or evidence the question asks for.
Example: one paragraph, two questions

Why Songbirds Fly at Night

Each spring and autumn, millions of small songbirds travel thousands of kilometers between their breeding grounds and their winter homes. Surprisingly, many of these birds, which are active only in daylight for the rest of the year, make their journeys at night.

[A] Night flight has clear physical advantages, because the air after sunset is usually cooler and calmer. [B] This helps the birds avoid overheating and losing too much water during hours of hard flying. [C] Darkness also offers protection, since many of the hawks that hunt small birds are active only during the day. [D] When the sun rises, the travelers land to rest and feed, rebuilding the fat reserves that will power the next night's flight.

Scientists are still studying how the birds find their way in the dark, but the stars and the Earth's magnetic field both seem to play a role.

On the test you would click the sentence in the passage. Here the four sentences of paragraph 2 are marked [A]–[D].

1. Identify the sentence in paragraph 2 that explains how night flight protects birds from predators.

Answer + Explanation

Correct Answer: C

Explanation

The question asks for one specific function: protection from predators. Sentence C is the only one that mentions an enemy ("hawks that hunt small birds") and explains why darkness helps (the hawks hunt only by day).

  • Choice A – it gives a physical advantage of night flight (cool, calm air), not protection from predators.
  • Choice B – it explains the benefit of cool air for the bird's body; no predators are mentioned.
  • Choice D – it describes what the birds do during the day, between flights.

2. Identify the sentence in paragraph 2 that describes what the birds do between flights.

Answer + Explanation

Correct Answer: D

Explanation

"Between flights" means during the day, when the birds are not flying. Sentence D says they land at sunrise to rest and feed, building fat for the next night.

  • Choice A – it is about conditions during the flight.
  • Choice B – it is about what happens during "hours of hard flying."
  • Choice C – it mentions the day, but only to explain when hawks hunt, not what the songbirds do.
