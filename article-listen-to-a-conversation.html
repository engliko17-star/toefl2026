Listen to a Conversation

In this task, you hear a short dialogue between two people, usually two students or a student and someone who works on campus. They talk about ordinary things: a class, a problem with a room, weekend plans, a job, food. After the conversation ends, you answer two questions about it.

The language is informal and natural: people joke, use idioms, don't finish their sentences and often say things indirectly. Understanding the words is not enough. You need to understand what the speakers mean, what they want and what they are going to do.

What you hearA conversation between two speakers (usually a man and a woman)
What you seeA picture of the two speakers. After the audio, the questions appear one at a time.
Length of the audio35–100 words, about 20–40 seconds, usually 4–8 turns
Questions per conversation2
Scored questions4 in Module 1 and 4 in Module 2 (on both paths)
Where in the sectionRight after the Choose a Response block, in both modules
LevelA2–C1
TopicsEveryday and campus life: classes and assignments, dining, shopping, housing, health, hobbies, entertainment, services, travel

Conversations are the only task type that appears in the same number in every module, so you will meet them no matter which path you are on.

What the Screen Looks Like

  • While the audio plays, you see only the picture. The questions appear after the conversation ends, so you don't know in advance what will be asked.
  • You answer question 1, click Next, and then see question 2. You can't go back to question 1 or replay the audio.
  • Some questions quote a short phrase from the conversation, for example What does the woman mean when she says, "…"? The quoted words are only a pointer: the question is about their meaning or purpose in the situation.

What This Task Tests

The big picture

Who is talking, where, and what the conversation is mainly about

Important details

Problems, reasons, times, places, decisions

Informal language

Idioms, colloquial phrases and the grammar that fluent speakers use in real talk

Implied meaning

What a speaker suggests without saying it directly

Purpose of a remark

Why a speaker says something: to refuse, to joke, to complain, to encourage

Predictions and connections

What the speakers will do next and how one turn responds to another

How a Conversation Is Built

Almost every TOEFL conversation follows the same simple shape. If you know the shape, you know where the answers usually are.

1 · Opening

A greeting or a question that sets the scene. "Is it just me, or is it freezing in here?"

2 · Problem or news

Something went wrong, changed or needs to be decided. Main idea questions come from here.

3 · Reaction and discussion

Opinions, reasons, jokes, suggestions. Detail, purpose and attitude questions come from here.

4 · Decision or next step

What they agree on or what someone will do. "What will … probably do next?" comes from here.

Note that the most important information is often at the very end. The last two lines usually contain the decision, and questions about the next step depend on them. Don't relax when you think the conversation is finishing.

Which Questions You Will Get

Conversation questions use the six standard Listening question types. These are the most common in conversations:

TypeTypical questions in conversations
Main IdeaWhat are the speakers mainly discussing? · What is the woman's problem?
FactualWhat does the man say about the new café? · Why is the woman late?
InferenceWhat can be inferred about the woman? · What will the man probably do next?
PurposeWhy does the woman say, "…"? · What does the man mean when he says, "…"?
AttitudeHow does the man feel about the change? · What is the woman's opinion of the class?
MethodRare in conversations, more typical of academic talks

"What does the speaker mean when he says, '…'?" is especially typical of conversations. It usually quotes an idiom, a joke or an indirect phrase, and the answer is what the phrase means in this situation.

Phrases That Carry the Meaning

Conversations are full of short set phrases. Their literal meaning is often different from what they mean in context, and questions like to test exactly these moments. Here are some that are worth knowing:

You hearWhat the speaker means
Tell me about it.I completely agree; I have the same problem. (Not a request for more information!)
You can say that again.I strongly agree.
I'll pass. / I think I'll sit this one out.No, thank you; I won't take part.
Count me in.Yes, I'll join.
I'm swamped. / I'm up to my ears in…I'm very busy.
I wouldn't count on it.It probably won't happen.
That's news to me.I didn't know that; I'm surprised.
It's not really my thing.I don't like it very much (a soft refusal).
I could use a break.I want / need a break.
No wonder…That explains it; now it makes sense.
What a relief!I was worried, and now I'm not.
I'd forget my head if it wasn't attached.I'm very forgetful (a joke about oneself).

Tone changes the meaning

"Oh, great." said happily means good news. Said flatly after hearing that the bus is cancelled, it means the opposite. "Sure." can be a real yes or an unconvinced one. Listen to how things are said, especially in the reaction to a problem.

Taking Notes on a Conversation

A conversation is short, so you don't need many notes. A simple frame is enough: who, the problem, the key reason or detail, and the decision. Split your page into M and W so you always know who said what.

Example notes for the practice conversation "Study room"
whoW = student · M = library desk problemW: room booked 4pm → system says TMRW ✗ (wrong date) detailsM: rm 12 free till 5, no projector · W: ok, only notes decisionM → switch to rm 12 + cancel tmrw

Common Traps

TrapWhat it looks like
Wrong speakerThe option is true, but it was the other person who said it or did it. Always check who the question is about.
Mentioned, but not the pointA detail that was mentioned in passing is presented as the main topic or as the reason.
Literal meaning of an idiom"I burn toast on a good day" → an option about a broken toaster. The idiom is about something else.
Words from the audioThe option uses exact words from the conversation but changes the meaning. The correct answer is usually a paraphrase.
A plan that changedThe first plan in the conversation, not the final one. Speakers often change their minds in the last lines.
Too much inferenceAn option that sounds reasonable but goes further than anything the speakers said.

Strategies for Listen to a Conversation

  • Identify the setting in the first line. Who are these people and where are they? Two friends in a dorm talk differently from a student and a librarian, and the questions will depend on their roles.
  • Ask "Why are they talking?" A problem to solve, a plan to make, a request, news to share? This is almost always one of the two questions.
  • Keep track of who says what. Wrong-speaker options are the most common trap in conversations.
  • Pay extra attention to the last two lines. Decisions, changes of plan and next steps are there.
  • Interpret, don't translate. When you hear an idiom or a joke, ask what it does in the conversation: agrees, refuses, complains, exaggerates?
  • Match meaning, not words. Choose the option that says the same thing in other words, and be careful with options that repeat what you heard word for word.
Example

You hear:

Narrator: Listen to a conversation between two students.

W: Are you going to Professor Grant's talk on Friday?

M: I was planning to, but my shift at the lab got moved to Friday afternoon.

W: Too bad. She's supposed to be really good. Couldn't you swap with someone?

M: I asked Dan, but he's away all weekend. Anyway, they're recording it, so I'll watch it online.

W: Well, if you want, I can take notes for you too.

M: That'd be great, thanks.

1. Why can't the man attend the talk?

AHe has to work at that time.
BHe does not like the speaker.
CHe will be away for the weekend.
DThe talk has been cancelled.

2. What does the woman offer to do?

ARecord the talk for the man
BTake the man's shift at the lab
CShare her notes from the talk
DAsk Dan to change his plans
Answers + Explanation

Correct Answers: 1 – A, 2 – C

Explanation

Question 1 (factual). The man says his shift at the lab was moved to Friday afternoon. "Has to work at that time" is a paraphrase of this, so A is correct.

  • B is the opposite of the conversation: the woman says the professor is supposed to be good, and the man wanted to go.
  • C is the wrong-speaker trap: it's Dan who is away for the weekend, not the man.
  • D is never mentioned. The talk is happening; it will even be recorded.

Question 2 (factual, from the last lines). The woman says "I can take notes for you", so C is correct.

  • A: the talk will be recorded, but by the organizers; the woman doesn't offer that.
  • B: she asks whether he could swap with someone, but she doesn't offer to do it herself.
  • D reuses the name Dan, but nobody suggests asking him again.

Conversation 1 of 4 · Study room

Narrator: Listen to a conversation at a university library.

Woman: Hi, I booked a study room for four o'clock, but the screen says it's taken.

Man: Let me check. Ah, I see. The system has your booking for tomorrow, not today.

Woman: Tomorrow? Oh no, I must have clicked the wrong date. And my group's already on the way.

Man: Well, room twelve is free until five, but it doesn't have a projector.

Woman: That's fine. We just need a table. We're only going over our notes.

Man: OK, I'll switch you over. And I'll cancel tomorrow's booking, unless you need that too.

Woman: No, you can cancel it. Thanks so much!

1. What is the woman's problem?

AHer group is going to be late.
BShe reserved a room for the wrong day.
CThe projector in her room does not work.
DShe left her notes at home.
Show answer

B. The man finds her booking for tomorrow, and she says she clicked the wrong date. B paraphrases this. A: her group is on the way, not late. C: a projector is mentioned, but only as something room twelve doesn't have. D: she mentions notes, but not that she forgot them.

2. What will the man probably do next?

ALook for a room with a projector
BCall the woman's group
CChange the woman's reservation
DBook room twelve for tomorrow
Show answer

C. At the end, the man says he will switch her to room twelve and cancel tomorrow's booking, and she agrees. That is changing her reservation. A: she says she doesn't need a projector. D mixes two details: room twelve is for today, and tomorrow's booking will be cancelled.

Conversation 2 of 4 · Cooking class

Narrator: Listen to a conversation between two students.

Man: Hey, did you see the flyer for the cooking class at the student center on Thursday?

Woman: The Italian one? I did. I'm tempted, but I burn toast on a good day.

Man: That's exactly why you should come! It's for total beginners.

Woman: Hmm. How much does it cost?

Man: It's free for students. You just have to sign up by Wednesday.

Woman: Free? Well, in that case, count me in. Could you sign up for both of us?

1. Why does the woman say, "I burn toast on a good day"?

ATo say that she is not good at cooking
BTo complain about the kitchen at the student center
CTo explain why she did not see the flyer
DTo suggest that they eat out instead
Show answer

A. Burning even simple toast is a joke that means she is a very poor cook. That's why the man answers that the class is for beginners. B takes the phrase literally. C and D are not connected to the phrase.

2. What makes the woman decide to take the class?

AShe loves Italian food.
BThe man offers to cook with her.
CThe class is only for beginners.
DIt does not cost anything.
Show answer

D. She hesitates and asks about the price. When she hears it's free, she says "in that case, count me in". So the price is what makes her decide. C is true, but she still wasn't convinced after hearing it. A and B are never said.

Conversation 3 of 4 · No heating

Narrator: Listen to a conversation between two roommates.

Woman: Is it just me, or is it freezing in here?

Man: It's not just you. The heating's been off since this morning.

Woman: Again? Did you call the building manager?

Man: Twice. He said someone would come sometime this afternoon.

Woman: That's what he said last week, and nobody showed up until Friday.

Man: I know. I'm starting to think we should put our complaint in writing.

1. What are the speakers mainly discussing?

AThe weather forecast for the week
BFinding a new building manager
CA problem with the heating in their apartment
DTheir plans for Friday
Show answer

C. The whole conversation is about the heating being off again and the manager not fixing it. A misreads "freezing" (it's about the room, not the weather). B: they complain about the manager but don't talk about replacing him. D: Friday is mentioned only as a detail from last week.

2. What does the woman imply about the building manager?

AHe does not keep his promises.
BHe started working there last week.
CHe fixed the heating quickly last time.
DHe only works on Fridays.
Show answer

A. She says he promised the same thing last week, and nobody came until Friday. She is suggesting that he doesn't do what he says. C is the opposite of what happened. B and D misuse "last week" and "Friday".

Conversation 4 of 4 · Flat tire

Narrator: Listen to a conversation between two students.

Man: You look exhausted. Long day?

Woman: Kind of. I started my job at the campus bookstore today, and I had to walk there because my bike has a flat tire.

Man: The bike shop next to the gym fixes flats while you wait. It only takes about ten minutes.

Woman: Really? I thought I'd have to leave it there for days.

Man: Nope. I had mine done there last month. Just go before they close at six.

Woman: It's already five fifteen. I'd better go right now.

1. Why is the woman tired?

AShe worked two shifts at the bookstore.
BShe stayed up late studying.
CShe rode her bike a long way.
DShe had to walk to her new job.
Show answer

D. She says she had to walk to the bookstore because of the flat tire. A: it was her first day, not two shifts. C is the opposite: she couldn't use her bike. B is never mentioned.

2. What will the woman probably do next?

ABuy a new bicycle
BTake her bike to the repair shop
CGo to the gym to exercise
DReturn to the bookstore
Show answer

B. The shop closes at six, it's five fifteen, and she says "I'd better go right now". She will go to the bike shop. C is a trap: the gym is only mentioned to show where the shop is.
