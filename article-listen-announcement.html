Listen to an Announcement

In this task, one person makes a short announcement, the kind you would hear at the start of a class, at a campus event, over a speaker in the library or on the student radio. After the announcement, you answer two questions about it.

Announcements are short and packed with practical information: what is happening, when, where, what changed and what listeners should do. The main challenge is to catch the purpose of the message and the key details at the same time, in one listening.

What you hearOne speaker: a professor, a campus staff member, a student leader or a radio host
What you seeA picture of the speaker. After the audio, the questions appear one at a time.
Length of the audio40–85 words, about 20–35 seconds
Questions per announcement2
Scored questions4 in Module 1; 4 in Module 2 Lower; none in Module 2 Upper
Where in the sectionAfter the conversations, before the academic talks
LevelA2–C1
ContentSchedules and changes, directions and locations, rules and policies, events, student achievements

Announcements and the two paths

Scored announcement questions appear in Module 1 and in the Lower Module 2, but not in the Upper one. If you are on the harder path, Module 1 is usually where you meet them. On the easier path, they are a quarter of all your scored points: 8 of 35.

What the Screen Looks Like

  • Before the audio, a short line tells you the setting, for example Listen to an announcement in a biology class or …on a university radio station. Use it: it tells you who the speaker is and who the audience is.
  • While the announcement plays, you see only the speaker. The questions appear after it ends, one at a time, and you can't go back.

What This Task Tests

Purpose and main idea

Why the announcement is being made and what it is mainly about

Key details

Times, dates, places, names, requirements

Implied information

What you can conclude from the message even though the speaker doesn't say it

What happens next

What listeners are expected to do, or what will happen because of the announcement

Kinds of Announcements

Most announcements belong to one of these types. Recognizing the type in the first sentence tells you what details to expect.

TypeTypical openingListen especially for
Schedule changeA quick change to our schedule…The old plan vs. the new one, and the reason for the change
Event / invitationI'm excited to let you know that…What the event is, when and where, who can come, whether you need to register
Rule or policyStarting Monday, the library will…What is now allowed or not allowed, where and when the rule applies, alternatives
Directions / locationFor those of you looking for…Buildings, floors, room numbers, landmarks
Reminder / deadlineJust a reminder that…The date, what must be done, what happens if you miss it
AchievementCongratulations to…Who achieved what, why it matters, any celebration or next step
Service problemDue to maintenance, …What is unavailable, for how long, and what to use instead

How an Announcement Is Built

1 · Attention

Good morning, everyone. / Attention, please. Tells you the audience.

2 · The news

One or two sentences that state the purpose. Main idea questions come from here.

3 · Details

When, where, why, who. Factual and inference questions come from here.

4 · Action

Please… / Make sure… / Don't forget… What listeners must do.

The purpose is almost always in the first two sentences, right after the greeting. The request for action is usually at the end. If you catch the beginning and the end, you already have material for most questions.

Signal Phrases to Listen For

You hearWhat comes next
I'd like to let you know… / I'm here to tell you about…The purpose of the announcement
…has been moved / postponed / cancelledA change: note both the old and the new information
instead / rather than / as usualThe contrast between the normal plan and the new one
due to / because of / sinceThe reason, often a question
please / make sure / be sure to / don't forgetWhat listeners must do, often a question
starting… / from now on / until further noticeWhen a new rule begins or how long it lasts
only / limited / required / optionalConditions and requirements: who can join, what is necessary
also / in addition / and one more thingA second piece of information, often tested

Which Questions You Will Get

TypeTypical questions about announcements
Main IdeaWhat is the announcement mainly about? · What is the main purpose of the announcement?
FactualWhere will the event take place? · What does the speaker ask students to do? · According to the speaker, why…?
InferenceWhat can be inferred about the workshop? · What problem is the speaker most likely addressing?

A typical pair: one question about the purpose of the whole announcement and one about a detail or a request.

Common Traps

TrapWhat it looks like
Old informationThe option gives the original time or place, which the speaker says has changed. The most frequent trap in announcements.
Wrong group of listenersAn instruction for volunteers is presented as an instruction for everyone, or the other way round.
Detail instead of purposeFor a main-purpose question, an option describes one detail (the room, the reason) instead of the whole message.
Numbers and times mixed upTwo numbers are mentioned (starts at three, register by Tuesday), and the option combines them wrongly.
Too strongThe speaker recommends something, and the option says it is required. Or some becomes all.
Mentioned word, wrong ideaThe option reuses a word from the audio (fire, weather) but builds a different meaning around it.

Strategies for Listen to an Announcement

  • Use the setting line. "In a biology class", "at a campus event", "on the student radio" tells you who is speaking and to whom.
  • Catch the purpose early. Ask yourself after the first two sentences: Why is this person speaking to us? Inform, remind, invite, warn, congratulate?
  • When you hear a change, write both versions. 210 → Harper. Questions love the new information, and wrong options love the old.
  • Listen for "please" and "make sure". The action the listeners must take is one of the most frequent questions.
  • Notice who each instruction is for. Volunteers…, If you signed up to…, First-year students…
  • Match meaning, not words. The correct option usually paraphrases: "bring your goggles, since Harper doesn't keep extras" becomes "bring their own safety equipment".
Example

You hear:

Narrator: Listen to an announcement from a university career center.

Good afternoon, students. This is a message from the Career Center. Our résumé workshop, originally scheduled for Wednesday, has been moved to Thursday at three, in room 105 of the Business Building. Space is limited to thirty people, so please register on the Career Center website by Tuesday evening. And bring a printed copy of your résumé. Our advisers will give you feedback on it during the session.

1. What is the main purpose of the announcement?

ATo announce a new day for a workshop
BTo explain how to write a résumé
CTo introduce new career advisers
DTo say that a workshop has been cancelled

2. What must students do in order to attend the workshop?

AArrive at the Business Building before three o'clock
BEmail their résumé to an adviser in advance
CSign up online by Tuesday evening
DVisit the Career Center on Wednesday
Answers + Explanation

Correct Answers: 1 – A, 2 – C

Explanation

Question 1 (main idea). The key sentence comes right after the greeting: the workshop has been moved from Wednesday to Thursday. Everything else (room, registration, résumé copy) is detail around this change. So A is correct.

  • B: the workshop is about résumés, but the announcement doesn't explain how to write one.
  • C: advisers are mentioned, but they aren't new and they are not the point.
  • D: moved is not cancelled. This option exploits students who hear "originally scheduled" and stop listening.

Question 2 (factual). The speaker says please register on the Career Center website by Tuesday evening. C paraphrases this: register on the website = sign up online.

  • A mixes up details: three o'clock is the start time, not a requirement to arrive earlier.
  • B: students should bring a printed copy, not email it.
  • D uses the old information: Wednesday was the original day of the workshop.

Announcement 1 of 4 · Lab room change

Narrator: Listen to an announcement in a chemistry class.

Good morning, everyone. Before we start, a quick change to the schedule. Next Tuesday's lab session won't be in room 210 as usual. The building is having its fire alarms tested that day, so we'll meet in the chemistry lab in Harper Hall instead. Same time, nine a.m. Please bring your own safety goggles, since Harper doesn't keep any extras. See you there.

1. What is the main purpose of the announcement?

ATo explain the fire safety rules on campus
BTo tell students where next Tuesday's lab will be held
CTo announce that a lab session has been cancelled
DTo introduce a new chemistry course
Show answer

B. The speaker announces a change: next Tuesday's lab moves from room 210 to Harper Hall. B states this purpose. A: the fire alarm test is only the reason for the change. C: the lab is moved, not cancelled. D is never mentioned.

2. What does the speaker ask students to do?

AArrive earlier than usual
BHelp test the fire alarms
CMeet in room 210 first
DBring their own safety goggles
Show answer

D. "Please bring your own safety goggles" is the only request. A: the time stays the same. B misuses the fire alarm detail. C is the old information: room 210 is exactly where they will not meet.

Announcement 2 of 4 · Debate team

Narrator: Listen to an announcement on a university radio station.

This is Campus Radio with your afternoon news. Congratulations to our debate team, who took second place at the national championship last weekend. That's the team's best result in over ten years. To celebrate, the team will hold an open practice debate this Friday at six in the student union, and everyone is welcome. Team captain Leo Park says they're also looking for new members for next season.

1. What is the announcement mainly about?

AThe success of a student team
BThe rules of a national competition
CNew opening hours at the student union
DThe election of a new team captain
Show answer

A. The announcement congratulates the debate team on its result and describes how they will celebrate. A sums this up. B: the championship is mentioned, but not its rules. C and D reuse "student union" and "captain".

2. What can be inferred about the debate team?

AIt won first place at the championship.
BIt will not compete next season.
CIt has not done this well in many years.
DIt practices only on Fridays.
Show answer

C. "The team's best result in over ten years" means that for more than ten years it hasn't done this well. A: the team took second place. B is the opposite: they're recruiting for next season. D: Friday is the day of one special event, not their only practice day.

Announcement 3 of 4 · Quiet hours

Narrator: Listen to an announcement at a university library.

Attention, please. Starting Monday, the library will begin its new quiet hours policy. From eight p.m. until closing, the second and third floors will be for silent study only. That means no group work and no phone calls on those floors. If you need to work with classmates, the group rooms on the first floor are still available, and you can reserve them online. Thank you for helping us keep the library a good place to study.

1. What change does the speaker announce?

AThe library will close earlier on weekdays.
BPhones will no longer be allowed anywhere in the library.
CGroup rooms must now be reserved in person.
DSome floors will be kept silent in the evenings.
Show answer

D. From 8 p.m., the second and third floors are for silent study. D paraphrases this. B is too strong: calls are banned only on two floors and only in the evening. A is never mentioned. C is the opposite: rooms are reserved online.

2. According to the speaker, where can students work in groups?

AIn rooms on the first floor
BIn the library café
COn the second floor after 8 p.m.
DIn any empty classroom
Show answer

A. The speaker says the group rooms on the first floor are still available. C describes exactly the place and time where group work is no longer allowed. B and D are never mentioned.

Announcement 4 of 4 · Food fair

Narrator: Listen to an announcement at a student club meeting.

Hi, everyone! Just a reminder that the International Food Fair is this Saturday from noon to four. Because of the rain in the forecast, we've moved it from the main lawn to the sports hall. Volunteers, please arrive by eleven to help set up the tables. And if you signed up to cook, remember to label any dishes that contain nuts. We're expecting a big crowd, so come hungry!

1. Why was the event moved?

AThe main lawn is being repaired.
BBad weather is expected.
CMore people signed up than expected.
DThe sports hall is closer to the kitchen.
Show answer

B. "Because of the rain in the forecast" gives the reason. B says the same in other words. C misuses "a big crowd", which is mentioned only at the end. A and D are never said.

2. What does the speaker ask students who are cooking to do?

AArrive by eleven o'clock
BMake enough food for a large crowd
CMark dishes that contain nuts
DBring their own tables
Show answer

C. "If you signed up to cook, remember to label any dishes that contain nuts". Label = mark, so C is correct. A is the wrong-audience trap: arriving by eleven is for volunteers. B turns "come hungry" into an instruction. D misuses "tables".

Review

Format

1 speaker · 40–85 words · heard once · 2 questions · scored in Module 1 and Lower Module 2.

Structure

Attention → the news (purpose) → details → what listeners must do.

Note frame

what · why · old → new · when / where · do!

Traps

Old information · wrong audience · detail instead of purpose · mixed-up numbers · "recommended" becomes "required".
