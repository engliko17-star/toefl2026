Listen and Repeat

In this task, you imagine you are being trained for a job on campus: guiding a tour, helping at the library desk, working at the registrar's office. Your trainer says 7 sentences, one at a time, and you repeat each sentence exactly, once, right after hearing it.

It sounds simple, but it tests a lot: whether you can process English quickly, hold a whole sentence in memory, and say it back clearly with natural rhythm. The sentences start short and end long and complex.

What you doHear a sentence, wait for the beep, repeat the sentence once
Number of sentences7, all in one scenario
Time to speak8 s (sentences 1–2) · 10 s (3–5) · 12 s (6–7)
PositionThe first task of the Speaking section (questions 1–7 of 11)
Scored onAccuracy (how closely you match the sentence) and intelligibility (how clearly you say it)
Points0–5 per sentence, 35 in total. Partial credit is given.
LevelA1–C2

How It Works on the Screen

  1. Introduction. A narrator reads the scenario aloud, and you see it in writing, for example: You are working at the campus library. You are being trained to help students at the front desk. Listen to your trainer and repeat what she says. Repeat only once.
  2. Sentence 1 plays immediately. The screen shows an illustration of the setting (a map, a building plan, a desk). Be ready: there's no extra warning.
  3. About 2 seconds of silence, then a beep. Start speaking only after the beep.
  4. A timer shows your remaining time. When it runs out, a message says your response is being saved, and the next sentence plays.
  5. Your position in the illustration moves forward with each sentence, like moving along a route on a tour.

7 Sentences, 3 Levels

The seven sentences always follow the same ladder of difficulty. Here is a library scenario as an example:

Short · sentences 1–2 9–11 syllables · one simple clause · 8 seconds

Books can be returned at the front desk.

Medium · sentences 3–5 about 14–16 syllables · prepositional phrases or a dependent clause · 10 seconds

Students who need a quiet space can go up to the third floor.

Long · sentences 6–7 19–23 syllables · several clauses, modifiers, lists · 12 seconds

Overdue books, which are charged a small daily fee, can be returned through the drop box outside.

The long sentences are where most points are lost. They are built with structures that overload memory: relative clauses (which are charged…), conditional clauses (If you can't find…), lists (a pool, a climbing wall, and a running track), and extra modifiers (right away, directly).

How Each Sentence Is Scored

Every repetition gets a score from 0 to 5. The scale is holistic: raters and the scoring engine look at the response as a whole and choose the level it fits best, so a response doesn't have to show every feature listed for its level. Below is the complete ETS scoring guide for this task, point by point, in our own words. The original wording is in The Official Guide to the TOEFL iBT® Test (2026 Pocket Edition), Chapter 5, "Listen and Repeat Task Scoring Guide".

  • It is fully intelligible and a word-for-word repetition of the prompt.

  • There are small changes in words or grammar that do not substantially change the meaning of the prompt.

For example:

  • One or two function words are missing or changed.
    • One content word is missing (in longer sentences) or replaced with a related word,

    • two words are in the wrong order.
  • One or two content words are unclear because of imprecise pronunciation. The speaker may correct themselves, but completes the response successfully.
3

A typical response shows the following:

  • The response contains most of the content words or ideas of the prompt.
    • Several function words may be changed or missing; one or more content words may be missing or changed in a way that matters.

2

An important part of the prompt is missing, and/or the response is very inaccurate.

A typical response shows the following:

  • A large part of the prompt is missing, and important parts of the original meaning are lost.

  • The response is not a complete, independent sentence; the meaning comes only in fragments.
  • Intelligibility is low: a listener who had not heard the prompt would have difficulty understanding it.
1

The response reproduces very little of the prompt, or it is mostly unintelligible.

A typical response shows the following:

  • Only a few words are said; most of the prompt is missing.
  • The response can be recognized as an attempt to repeat the prompt, but most of it cannot be understood.

Accuracy

How many words you repeat correctly and how close the whole is to the original.

Intelligibility

Correct pronunciation, natural rhythm and word stress.

Fluency

Speaking rate, long runs of words without pauses, few hesitations.

These are the features the ETS scoring engine analyzes for this task, according to the TOEFL Technical Manual.

What the scale tells you

The biggest drops happen when a response stops being a full sentence (3 → 2) and when the meaning changes (4 → 3). A small slip like activity instead of activities costs only one point. So when you are unsure, keep the sentence complete and keep the meaning, even if a small word goes missing.

How to Hold a Long Sentence in Memory

Nobody can remember 20 separate syllables. Fluent speakers remember chunks of meaning, usually 3–5 words that go together, and the sentence is just 3–4 chunks:

Overdue books, which are charged a small daily fee, can be returned through the drop box outside.

Four pictures, not twenty syllables: late books · small fee each day · can return · box outside.

  • Listen for meaning, not for sounds. If you understand the sentence, you can rebuild it. That's why vocabulary and grammar practice improve this task too.
  • Keep the skeleton. Who does what: books · can be returned · through the drop box. Hang the extra parts on it.
  • Copy the music. Repeat with the same stress and pauses as the speaker. Pausing between chunks sounds natural; pausing inside a chunk sounds unsure.
  • Mind the small words. a, the, to, can, should, endings like -s and -ed. They are the most common reason for a 4 instead of a 5.

Common Mistakes

MistakeWhy it costs points
Starting before the beepThe beginning of your answer may not be recorded.
Stopping after a mistakeA stopped sentence is a fragment (2 points or less). A finished one with an error can still get 4.
Repeating twice or restartingThe instruction is to repeat once. Starting over wastes the time for the long sentences.
Using your own wordsSynonyms and paraphrases keep the meaning but lower accuracy. The task is exact repetition, not retelling.
Adding words"So, students who…" or "Um, OK" at the start. Extra words are changes too.
RacingSpeaking very fast to "get it out" before you forget reduces clarity.
Dropping endingsreturn for returned, book for books. Small, but it moves a 5 to a 4.

Strategies for Listen and Repeat

  • Use the scenario to predict. In a library, expect books, fees, printers and rooms. Knowing the topic makes words easier to catch and remember. If the picture doesn't help you, ignore it and just listen.
  • Wait for the beep, then start without delay. Use the 2-second pause to "replay" the sentence silently in your head.
  • Finish every sentence. If you lose a part, continue with what you remember, especially the end. Partial credit is given for every correct part.
  • Say it once, calmly. It's fine to finish early and have silence at the end of the recording.
  • Don't write. There's no time for notes, and writing stops you from hearing the sentence as a whole.
  • Train daily with real audio. Pause a podcast, audiobook or video after a sentence and repeat it exactly. Start with short sentences and work up to long ones.

Practice: Test Simulation

This works like the real task: 7 sentences, each played once, a beep, and 8, 10 or 12 seconds to repeat. The sentences are hidden until the end. Then you can compare your recordings with the original and see the text.

The interactive simulator needs JavaScript. You can still practice with a partner: they read each sentence aloud once, and you repeat it after a short pause.

Campus tour: sentences for your partner

You are training to be a campus tour guide. Listen to your trainer and repeat what he says. Repeat only once.

  1. This is the main building of the college.
  2. The library is open every day.
  3. The science labs are located behind the student center.
  4. You can get lunch at the café next to the art museum.
  5. Most first-year students live in the dorms across the river.
  6. The gym, which was renovated last year, has a pool, a climbing wall, and a running track.
  7. If you have any questions after the tour, the admissions office is open until five.
Fitness center desk: sentences for your partner

You are working at the front desk of the campus fitness center. Listen to your trainer and repeat what she says. Repeat only once.

  1. Please scan your student card at the front gate.
  2. Towels are available at the desk.

  5. Students can borrow lockers for the day if they bring a lock.
