Talk About Personal Preferences or Interests

In this type of question, the interviewer asks what you like, enjoy or value: your hobbies, your tastes, the choices you usually make, and why. It belongs to the group of personal questions in the Take an Interview task.

GroupPersonal questions
Where in the interviewEarly. According to the Official Guide, the first questions are personal; later questions are abstract or speculative.
Official example"What is one thing you did in school that you are proud of? Why are you proud of it?"
Time45 seconds, no preparation
Scoring0–5, the Take an Interview scoring guide (relevance, elaboration, delivery, language use)

How to Recognize It

These questions ask about your tastes and choices, and often also ask why. Typical wordings:

  • What do you enjoy doing in your free time?
  • Do you prefer … or …? Why?
  • What kind of … do you like most?
  • What are you interested in outside of class?
  • What is one thing you are proud of? Why?
  • What do you like about …?

Note the official example: it asks what and then why. The why part is where most of the elaboration comes from.

This type: your personal choice

Do you prefer studying alone or with friends?

A different type: a general opinion

Do you think students learn better in groups or individually? This is Share Opinions on Topics and Trends (the Official Guide's example for that type).

The Scoring Guide, Applied to This Question Type

The four dimensions of the Take an Interview scoring guide mean the following for an answer about your preferences:

DimensionStronger answer (4–5)Weaker answer (2–3)
RelevanceNames a clear preference and answers every part of the question (what and why)Names a preference but skips the why; drifts to another topic
ElaborationExplains the reasons with concrete details or a short example from your lifeOnly lists likes: "I like music, sports and movies." Little development
DeliveryA steady conversational pace; pauses sound naturalFrequent or long pauses, a choppy pace, many fillers
Language useVaried, accurate ways to express likes, preferences and reasonsLimited grammar and vocabulary clearly restrict what can be said

Grammar for Likes and Preferences

Repeating "I like… I like… I like…" shows a narrow range. These patterns let you express the same idea in more precise ways:

PatternUseExample
enjoy / love / don't mind + -ingLiking an activity (verb + -ing, not to)I really enjoy cooking for friends.
prefer A to B / prefer -ing to -ingA general preferenceI prefer paper books to e-books.
would rather + verb (than …)A preference in a particular situationI'd rather study at home than in the library.
be into / be interested in / be a fan ofInterestsLately I've been into photography.
What I like most about … is …Focusing on the reasonWhat I like most about hiking is the quiet.
ComparativesExplaining a choiceIt's more relaxing and a lot cheaper.
Adverbs of frequencyHow often you do itI usually go running, but I rarely go to the gym.
be proud of + noun / -ingAchievements (the official example)I'm proud of organizing our school's first science fair.

Official Guide strategies that matter most here

  • Answer each question directly. If the question asks you to choose between two options, make your choice clear.
  • Elaborate with details, examples or reasoning. Give the reasons for your preference, or share a brief story that shows it.
  • If you are unsure, answer from your own experience or opinion. Partial credit is given for any relevant information.
  • Use the full 45 seconds. If you finish early, add more details or examples.
  • Use varied vocabulary and accurate grammar, and keep going if you make a mistake.
  • No memorized language. A prepared speech about your hobbies is exactly the kind of answer that is scored lower.

Practice: 8 Questions About Preferences and Interests

Two sets of four questions, all of this type, in test conditions: each question is asked once, and your 45 seconds start right after it. Afterwards, listen to your answers and check them against the scoring guide. The questions were written for this platform in the style of the task; they are not official test questions.

The interactive simulator needs JavaScript. You can still practice with a partner: they read each question once, and you answer for 45 seconds with a timer.

Campus life survey: questions for your partner

The student union is running a survey about how students spend their free time. A member of the student union will ask you some questions.

  1. Thanks for helping with our survey. What do you usually enjoy doing in your free time, and why?
  2. Thank you. Do you prefer spending time with a big group of friends or with just one or two people? Why?
  3. Interesting. What kind of music, films or books do you enjoy most? What do you like about them?
  4. Great. Is there a subject or activity you have become interested in recently? What got you interested in it?
Library study habits: questions for your partner

The university library is doing a study on how students like to study and relax. A librarian will interview you online.

  1. Hello, and thanks for joining. Where do you prefer to study: at home, in the library, or somewhere else? Why?
  2. Thank you. Do you prefer reading on paper or on a screen? Why?
  3. I see. What is your favorite way to take a break when you are studying?
  4. Last question. Is there a hobby you would recommend to other students? What makes it worthwhile?

Review

Recognize

What do you enjoy… · Do you prefer… · What are you proud of… About your tastes and choices. Early in the interview.

Include

A clear preference, the reasons for it, and a concrete detail or example. Every part of the question.

Language

enjoy + -ing · prefer A to B · would rather · be into · What I like most is… · comparatives.
